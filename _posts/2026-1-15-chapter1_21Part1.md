<h2 style="color:#3ba2a6; font-family: 'Press Start 2P', 'system-ui'"><b>x86</b></h2>

<p>
The author began this section by saying that there is a special instruction called <code style="color: chartreuse;">LOOP</code> in the x86 instruction set.
</p>
<p>
This instruction checks the value in the <code style="color: chartreuse;">ECX</code> register; if it is not zero, it decrements ECX by one and transfers control to the label specified in the LOOP operand.
</p>
<p>
Generally, this instruction is not very convenient, and modern compilers do not generate it automatically.
</p>
<p>
Therefore, if you see this instruction in code, it is highly likely that the code is hand-written Assembly.
</p>
<p>
In C/C++, loops are usually implemented using:
</p>
<ul>
  <li><code style="color: chartreuse;">for()</code></li>
  <li><code style="color: chartreuse;">while()</code></li>
  <li><code style="color: chartreuse;">do/while()</code></li>
</ul>
<p>
Let us start with <code style="color: chartreuse;">for()</code>.
</p>
<p>
This statement specifies:
</p>
<ul>
  <li>Loop initialization (setting the initial value of the counter)</li>
  <li>Loop condition (is the counter greater or less than a certain limit?)</li>
  <li>What happens at each iteration (increment or decrement)</li>
  <li>And of course, the loop body</li>
</ul>
<p>
The general form:
</p>

<p>
Now let us see what happens when we enable optimization (/Ox):
</p>
<p>
What happened here is slightly different: no stack space was allocated for the variable <code style="color: chartreuse;">i</code>, and the <code style="color: chartreuse;">ESI</code> register was used exclusively for it. This is possible in small functions with few local variables.
</p>
<p>
Another very important point: the <code style="color: chartreuse;">printing_function()</code> function must not change the value of <code style="color: chartreuse;">ESI</code>. The compiler is confident of this. If the compiler decided to use <code style="color: chartreuse;">ESI</code> inside <code style="color: chartreuse;">printing_function()</code>, it would have to save its value at the beginning and restore it at the end, as we saw with <code style="color: chartreuse;">PUSH ESI / POP ESI</code>.
</p>

Here the situation changed: the loop was completely unrolled (loop unrolling). This has the advantage of saving execution time by removing loop control instructions, but at the cost of larger code size.
</p>
<p>
Large fully unrolled loops are not recommended nowadays because large functions require more instruction cache space.
</p>

</p>

</p>
<p>

</p>
<p>
The following <code style="color: chartreuse;">BLT</code> (Branch Less Than) instruction jumps if <code style="color: chartreuse;">i</code> is less than 10.
</p>
<p>
Otherwise, 0 is written to <code style="color: chartreuse;">R0</code> (return value) and execution finishes.
</p>
